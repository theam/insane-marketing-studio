# The infomercial style ("slopcore late-night")

The INSANE Shepherdr infomercial: a 90s late-night infomercial made by someone who has spent too long online. Loud,
absurd, nerdy, cheap on purpose, and precise underneath: every gag lands on its word.

## Structure

1. **The problem**, the viewer's, played big, in **black and white on a grey studio set**, to sad detuned muzak.
2. **"There has to be a better way!"** (a record scratch, the music stops).
3. **"Introducing…"** → the name **on fire**, its name repeated at every edge in party colours, explosions, a flash.
   Colour floods in and the music explodes into synth-funk.
4. **How it works**: one feature per line, one cut each, a cut-out and a badge, escalating ("Ten! A hundred! No… A
   THOUSAND!").
5. **"But wait, there's more!"** (optional).
6. **The offer**: "How much would you pay? A thousand dollars? A TRILLION DOLLARS?" — the price tag flips, explodes
   on a laser show — "It's free." — a red slash and a FREE stamp.
7. **The call to action**, then a deadpan disclaimer: "Results not typical."
8. **The end card**: the name, the URL, the credits; fade; full black.

## Look

- **Impact, uppercase, white with a 16 px black stroke**, as big as it fits; **WordArt** (stacked offset copies in
  red, amber, green, cyan, blue) for the peaks.
- **Real photographic cut-outs**, centred, often with a **white sticker outline**; raining or flying cut-outs for the
  biggest moments. Never drawings.
- **Backgrounds**: the phosphor CRT grid rushing to a banded pixel sun (green normally, amber for money, cyan for
  scale, rainbow-glitch for the peaks), a laser show, a kaleidoscope tunnel, the grey/blue studio set.
- **8-bit stickers** (Press Start 2P), **rubber stamps**, red Xs and slashes, comic speech bubbles, starbursts.
- **Fire walls and columns, explosions** (photos on black, screened in), always at the sides of the words.
- **Scanlines and a vignette** over everything; captions in a small monospace bar at the bottom.

## Motion

- Everything lands **on its word**, in a 2-frame step (overshoot 1.12, then 1.05, then 1): no smooth easing.
- Scales snap to 1/40 steps ("pixel stepped"); a slow 4 % push while a cut holds.
- **Shouted (CAPITALISED) words punch the frame**: a zoom and a shake in 4 px steps.
- Physical, exaggerated motion: things fall in, fly across, explode. Effects on stills stand in for video.

## Sound

- A narrator from a 90s toy commercial (Voice Commander Xtreme), time-stretched 1.32×, broadcast chain, gaps of
  0.05-0.15 s: **frantic**.
- A bed that changes with every section and runs the whole video.
- Effects on every gag, levelled by family, **nothing shrill**.

## Tone

Nerdy, self-aware, absurd escalation with current memes; never sexualized; never copying the thing it parodies.
